What this is

An early-warning system for neighborhood-level stories across New York City. It scans nine data sources and synthesizes them into story leads for each of the city's 59 community districts.

The premise is simple: local stories don't appear out of nowhere. Before a neighborhood concern becomes a media story, it leaves traces across multiple city systems. This tool surfaces those traces.

What each source tells you

311 complaints tell you what is being reported — the raw signal of resident frustration, tested for statistical anomalies.
Council constituent cases tell you what is politically sticky — issues residents have escalated to their elected officials.
CB budget requests tell you what districts keep asking the city to fund — the same request year after year means the problem hasn't been solved.
Land use / ULURP applications tell you what is about to become a fight — rezonings, shelter sitings, street redesigns that haven't hit the news yet.
DOB building permits tell you where the physical landscape is changing — spikes in new building or demolition filings signal development pressure.
Community board meetings tell you what is becoming organized — when an issue gets on a committee agenda, someone is trying to do something about it.
Reddit & local news tell you what the public conversation looks like — whether an issue has broken through or is still simmering.

The convergence signal

The most valuable output is convergence. When 311 complaint spikes, community board committee topics, and council constituent cases all point at the same corner, corridor, school zone, or housing complex — that is often the moment before the issue graduates into a media story.

How scoring works

Each story lead is scored on severity, verifiability, freshness, human interest, data richness, and editorial potential. Leads that appear across multiple data sources get higher scores. Districts are colored by an activity score (0–100) combining complaint volume, spike severity, and category diversity.

Sources

311 complaints, CB budget requests, land use applications, council constituent cases, and DOB permits come from NYC Open Data (Socrata API). Community board meetings are scraped from nyc.gov. Reddit posts come from public subreddit feeds. Local news comes from RSS feeds of 11 outlets including THE CITY, Gothamist, Hell Gate, and City Limits. Every data point links back to its source.
